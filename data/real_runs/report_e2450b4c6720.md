# Research Report

**Question:** What is reranking and why are cross-encoders used?

## Executive Summary

Reranking is a crucial step in information retrieval that enhances the precision of search results by reordering a set of candidate documents based on their relevance to a query. Cross-encoders are preferred for reranking tasks due to their ability to evaluate the query and document pairs together, providing more accurate relevance scores.

## Definition of Reranking

- Reranking is defined as the precision step that follows the recall step in information retrieval, where a rough first set of results is reordered using a more accurate model to ensure the best matches for a query are prioritized. [1]
- Reranking is the second stage of a two-stage retrieval pipeline, where a slower and more accurate model rescoring each query and document pair together improves the precision of the final result set. [2]

## Role of Cross-Encoders in Reranking

- Cross-encoders have become the standard machinery for reranking since the introduction of models like BERT, as they allow for the simultaneous encoding of query and document pairs, leading to more accurate relevance scoring. [3]
- Cross-encoders are preferred for reranking tasks because they provide higher accuracy, especially in scenarios where subtle differences in wording can significantly impact the meaning, such as in medical or legal documents. [4]

## Applications of Reranking and Cross-Encoders

- Reranking models, such as the NVIDIA NeMo Retriever Reranking NIM, play a key role in retrieval processes by reordering citations based on their relevance to a query, which is essential for applications that involve multiple datastores with different similarity algorithms. [5]
