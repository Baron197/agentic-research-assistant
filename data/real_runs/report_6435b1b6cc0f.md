# Research Report

**Question:** What is RAG?

## Executive Summary

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a process that enhances the capabilities of large language models by integrating external authoritative knowledge bases, allowing for more accurate and contextually relevant responses.

## Definition of RAG

- RAG stands for Retrieval-Augmented Generation, which is a method that optimizes the output of large language models by referencing external knowledge bases before generating responses. [1]

## Contexts of RAG Usage

- RAG is commonly used in contexts where the model needs to behave predictably and where the retrieval of information is governed by specific constraints. [2][3]

## Key Features of RAG

- Key features of RAG include the ability to provide current and up-to-date knowledge by relying on external knowledge bases, which helps in generating more accurate responses. [4][5]

## Comparison with Similar Concepts

- RAG differs from fine-tuning in that it uses an organization's internal data to enhance prompt engineering without the need for retraining the model. [6]

## Benefits and Drawbacks of RAG

- One significant advantage of RAG is its ability to reduce hallucinations in language models by relying on a retrieval system to provide factual and up-to-date information. [7]
- RAG accuracy problems can arise from failures at various stages of the RAG pipeline, particularly when the underlying knowledge base is ungoverned or outdated. [8]
